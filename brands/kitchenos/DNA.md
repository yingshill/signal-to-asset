# Brand DNA: KitchenOS

> A smart-home cooking app that brings restaurant-grade technique and systems into the home kitchen.
> Position: your operating system for cooking like a pro at home — chef-level method made approachable, never intimidating.

---

## Voice & Tone
- **Warm + approachable, with quiet credibility.** Talk like a great chef-friend coaching you through it — encouraging, never condescending. Lower the intimidation barrier first; the expertise shows through, it isn't shouted.
- **Demystify, don't dumb down.** Every technique has a *why*. Explain the reasoning (heat, salt, timing, mise en place) so the home cook learns the system, not just the recipe.
- **Confidence without snobbery.** We respect restaurant craft but refuse food-world gatekeeping. No "you're doing it wrong" — instead "here's the move that makes it easier."
- **Calm, system-minded.** KitchenOS is about *order* — prep, sequence, repeatability. Copy should feel as organized as a well-run line, but read as friendly.
- **Show, then reassure.** Pair the pro move with the safety net: "If it splits, here's the fix." Builds trust and lowers stakes.

## Target Audience
- **Ambitious home cooks (primary buyer).** Cook several times a week, follow food media, own decent gear, want to level up from "good" to "restaurant-good." Frustrated by recipes that skip the *why*.
- **Aspiring / prosumer cooks.** Hosting dinner parties, batch-prepping, chasing a few signature dishes. Intimidated by pro technique — KitchenOS removes the fear.
- **Chefs & culinary pros (aspiration + credibility anchor).** Not the core buyer, but their endorsement and methods are the brand's north star. Featuring their systems validates the product for home cooks.
- **Geo / platform skew:** EN-speaking global home cooks (Instagram) + CN urban food enthusiasts (Xiaohongshu). Skew 25–45, food-curious, gear-aware, time-pressed.

## Content Pillars
1. **Technique decoded** — one pro move per piece (sear, emulsify, pan sauce, knife work), with the *why* and the home-kitchen adaptation. Evergreen, save-worthy.
2. **Systems & mise en place** — prep flow, batching, sequencing, kitchen organization. The "OS" pillar: cooking as a repeatable system, not chaos.
3. **Recipe walkthroughs** — restaurant dishes made achievable at home, broken into KitchenOS steps. Showcases the app in action.
4. **Gear & setup** — tools, ingredients, and kitchen setup that unlock pro results. Reasoned picks with price-tier alternatives; affiliate-ready.

## Default Channels (overrides global LinkedIn-centric defaults)
- **Instagram carousel** — primary EN channel for technique-decoded and recipe walkthroughs. 5–8 slides, visual-led, strong save/share intent.
- **Instagram Reel script** — short-form for single techniques and "before/after" transformations. 15–45 sec.
- **Xiaohongshu post** — primary CN channel. Cover image + narrative body. Written natively in Chinese, not translated.
- **Notion Website entry** — evergreen archive / recipe + technique library that social posts link back to.

Optional/approval-gated: LinkedIn (for fundraising/B2B founder narrative), TikTok, YouTube, email newsletter.

## Anti-patterns (do NOT do)
- Don't gatekeep or shame ("real chefs would never…"). The brand removes intimidation, never adds it.
- Don't skip the *why* — a technique with no reasoning is just another recipe.
- Don't over-claim the app replaces skill; it's a system that *builds* skill.
- Don't translate CN/EN word-for-word — rewrite natively for each platform.
- Don't recommend gear or ingredients we wouldn't use ourselves; always give a budget alternative.
- Don't drift into pure food-influencer aesthetics with no teaching substance.
